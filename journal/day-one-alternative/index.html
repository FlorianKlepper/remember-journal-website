The story behind Remember

Why I built a journaling app
without a subscription

Remember is built by an indie studio from the Bavarian Alps. I've been keeping a journal on and off for years, but I always found myself wanting something that the existing apps couldn't give me: a way to see not just what I did, but where.

I travel a lot within Germany and Europe — weekend hikes in the Alps, spontaneous road trips, city weekends with friends. After a while, my journal entries started blurring together. I'd write about a great hike, but couldn't remember exactly which trail it was. I'd log a restaurant, but forget which city I was in. What I needed wasn't just text — I needed a map.

So I built Remember. Every entry is tied to a GPS location, and all your entries show up as pins on an interactive Apple Maps view. You can zoom out and see your entire life as a map — and then zoom in on any place and find every memory you created there.

I also made a deliberate decision not to use a subscription model. I find subscription pricing for personal tools increasingly frustrating. A journaling app is something intimate — something you use every day to record your life. It should feel like something you own, not something you rent. Remember is a one-time purchase. That's it. Future updates are included. There are no annual renewals.

Remember is a one-person indie project, built and maintained by me alone. If you have feedback, a feature request, or a bug report, you can email me directly at support@remember-journal.com — and I'll actually read and reply. That's the kind of indie product I want to build, and the kind of app I'd want to use myself.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Everything Day One users want to know before switching to a journaling app without a subscription.

Can I import my Day One journal entries into Remember?

There is currently no direct import from Day One into Remember. Remember uses a different data structure built around GPS locations, activity categories and map pins — it's a fundamentally different approach to journaling. Most users start fresh in Remember and find it a welcome new chapter. Your existing Day One entries remain accessible in Day One at any time — you don't have to delete or lose anything.

Does Remember have cloud sync?

Yes. Remember uses Apple iCloud to sync your data across your devices if iCloud Drive is enabled. This means your entries are backed up automatically without any third-party server involved. Everything stays within Apple's ecosystem, which is GDPR compliant. There is no separate Remember account, no Remember cloud, and no data sent to our servers — because we don't have any.

Is there a Mac or web version of Remember?

Remember is currently iPhone-only (iOS 17+). A Mac companion app is on the roadmap. For now, the focus is making the iPhone experience exceptional — location-based journaling naturally belongs in your pocket. You always have your phone with you when you experience something; you can't say the same about your laptop.

What about my privacy? Is Remember GDPR compliant?

Privacy is a core part of Remember's design. The app is made by an indie developer in Germany and is fully GDPR compliant. All journal data is stored locally on your iPhone. There is no advertising, no behavioral tracking, no analytics selling your data to third parties, and no account creation required. The only optional data sharing is Apple iCloud sync — and that's controlled entirely by your iPhone's iCloud settings.

Why does Remember not use a subscription?

This is a deliberate philosophy decision. Subscriptions create ongoing financial pressure that often pushes apps to add features you don't need — just to justify the monthly cost. Remember is built on the belief that a journaling app should be something you own. Pay once, use forever. If a truly major new version ever requires a new purchase, you'll be informed well in advance and have the choice. There are no surprise charges and no automatic renewals.

Is the upgrade really a one-time payment — no hidden fees?

Yes, completely. You pay once through the App Store and Remember Unlimited is yours without any recurring charges. The free version supports up to 100 entries, which is enough to genuinely evaluate the app. The one-time upgrade unlocks unlimited entries forever. There are no monthly fees, no annual renewals, no premium tier above Unlimited, and no in-app advertising. The App Store shows the exact price for your country before you buy.

What happens when I reach 100 free entries?

The app will notify you when you approach the limit and offer the one-time unlock. You can still view and edit all your existing entries at any time — nothing is deleted or locked away. You simply can't add new entries until you unlock unlimited access. 100 entries is a meaningful amount — it covers several months of regular journaling and gives you a real sense of whether Remember fits your daily routine.

Can I export my journal data from Remember?

Yes. Remember includes export functionality that lets you save your journal as PDF or CSV. This means your memories are never locked into a proprietary format — you always own your data in a form you can read, print, share or import elsewhere. Data portability is important, especially for something as personal as a journal.

Does Remember work without an internet connection?

Yes, mostly. You can create entries, write text, add photos, assign categories and record GPS coordinates entirely offline. The map view requires an internet connection to load map tiles from Apple Maps. GPS location recording works without internet. When you come back online, iCloud sync happens automatically in the background without you having to do anything.

Is Remember actively maintained? Will there be future updates?

Yes. Remember is actively developed by RememberApps Studio. Updates are released regularly and include bug fixes, performance improvements and new features. As a one-person indie project, development moves at a thoughtful pace — quality over quantity. You can always reach the developer directly at support@remember-journal.com with feedback, feature requests, or bug reports. Responses are personal, not automated.
